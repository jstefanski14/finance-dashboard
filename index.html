Key considerations

Converting before your October wedding is advantageous — you're filing single at a lower effective rate. After marriage, MFJ brackets are wider but combined income (~$162k) still lands in the 22% bracket.

The Rollover IRA creates a pro-rata rule problem for backdoor Roth contributions. Eliminating it via conversion also unblocks the backdoor Roth strategy once your income exceeds direct Roth limits.

Best practice: convert in tranches that keep you within your current bracket. At $112,500 salary single, your 22% bracket runs to ~$95,375 taxable income — leaving some room for modest conversions each year.

Critical: deferred interest vs. true 0%

Deferred interest (common at Best Buy, mattress stores): if you carry any balance when the promo ends, ALL backdated interest from purchase date is added instantly. On $2,400 at 29.99% APR for 12 months that could be ~$600+ in a single day.

True 0% APR: only interest going forward from expiry date. Confirm which you have by calling the issuer or checking your original agreement. Set a calendar reminder 45 days before expiry to pay off the balance in full.
